# Study Guide Rules

Every finished set of notes ends with a study guide. The notes themselves organize the material; the study guide tells the student where to spend their time.

The study guide is part of the same document. Do not create a separate file for it unless asked.

## Required Sections

### 1. Named Frameworks With Numbered Steps

The most testable category in most material. List them in a table with the step count and where they appear.

| Framework | Steps | Where |
|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [n] | §[section] |

Step counts belong in this table because "how many steps does X have" is a common question format.

### 2. Direct Comparisons

Every two-way or three-way comparison in the material, listed as a bulleted set. The student should be able to see at a glance every "A vs. B" they need to know both sides of.

### 3. Specific Numbers

A table of every concrete figure in the material — thresholds, counts, percentages, ages, totals, years. Highlight the number cell.

| Number | What it refers to |
|---|---|

Numbers scattered through prose are easy to lose. Collected in one table, they become a five-minute review.

### 4. Definitions Most Likely To Be Asked Outright

A single run-on list of defined terms, separated by middle dots. This is a checklist, not an explanation — the definitions themselves live in the body of the notes.

### 5. Cross-References To Other Material

When the student has provided more than one chapter or source, include a table showing where concepts overlap.

| Concept | How [other source] treats it |
|---|---|

Overlapping concepts are likely targets for questions that span multiple chapters, and the student should study them once rather than twice.

### 6. Gaps and Items To Verify

Anything the analysis could not resolve, formatted as a distinct callout. Use a clear tag for each:

- **MISSING FROM THE SOURCE** — an outline or objective names a topic the material never covers
- **AMBIGUOUS WORDING** — the source says something that conflicts with the standard meaning of a term
- **DO NOT ASSUME** — the source gives an attribute for one side of a comparison but not the other

Never fill these gaps by guessing. Never quietly drop them either. Surface them so the student can ask.

## Confirmed vs. Possible

If the student has provided no confirmed test information, open the study guide by saying so plainly:

> No confirmed test material was provided. Everything here reflects what appears especially testable based on how the material presents it — not confirmed exam content.

Follow `TEST_PRIORITY.md` for the distinction. Never let a study guide imply certainty the student did not give you.

## Optional Additions

Include these when the material supports them, not by default:

- **Memory hooks** — a short mnemonic or contrast that makes a distinction stick
- **Practice suggestions** — a concrete self-test, especially for worked calculations
- **Pattern notes** — the underlying logic of a sequence, so the student can reconstruct steps they forget rather than relying on recall alone
