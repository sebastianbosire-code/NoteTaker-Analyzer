# Test Priority Rules

The student may provide information about what will be on an exam, quiz, or test.

This information should affect how the notes are analyzed and formatted.

## 1. Confirmed Test Material

Use:

> 🟥 **TEST PRIORITY**

for material that has been confirmed by:

- the student
- the professor
- a study guide
- an exam review
- explicit wording in supplied course material

Examples of confirmation:

- "The professor said this is on the test."
- "Know chapters 4 and 5."
- "Memorize this formula."
- "There will be a question about this."
- "This section is heavily tested."

## 2. What To Do With Confirmed Test Material

Pay extra attention to:

- definitions
- formulas
- variables
- numbers
- dates
- frameworks
- lists
- steps
- processes
- comparisons
- exceptions
- cause-and-effect relationships
- professor examples
- terminology

Do not over-compress confirmed test material.

## 3. Possible Test Material

Claude may notice material that appears especially testable.

Examples:

- a named framework
- a definition
- a formula
- a repeated concept
- a list of steps
- an important comparison
- a model
- a major process
- something heavily emphasized in the material

Use:

> 🟨 **POSSIBLE TEST MATERIAL**

This means the material appears important or testable, but it has **not** been confirmed.

## 4. Never Pretend To Know

Never say:

"This will be on your test"

unless it was explicitly confirmed in the supplied information.

Instead say:

"This appears especially testable"

or use:

> 🟨 **POSSIBLE TEST MATERIAL**

## 5. User-Provided Test Focus Before Analysis

The student may tell you what to focus on before uploading or analyzing material.

Example:

> My professor said EOQ, safety stock, and inventory turnover will be on the exam.

Treat any matching sections in the supplied material as:

> 🟥 **TEST PRIORITY**

Give those sections more detail and make them visually stand apart.

## 6. Test Priority Within Normal Notes

Do not create a completely separate document unless requested.

Keep test-priority material in the correct location within the notes so the surrounding context still makes sense.

Example:

## 5. Inventory Management

General explanation...

> 🟥 **TEST PRIORITY**
>
> **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ):** ...
>
> **Formula:** ...

Continue normal notes afterward.

## 7. Color Limitation

Markdown and Claude may not reliably display custom text colors.

Use visual markers instead:

- 🟥 Confirmed test priority
- 🟨 Possible test material

These markers should consistently stand apart from normal notes.
