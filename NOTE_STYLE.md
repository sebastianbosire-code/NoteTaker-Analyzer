# Note Style Guide

These rules define how finished notes should look.

The preferred style is:

**structured, visual, concise, easy to scan, and easy to understand**

The goal is not to make notes as short as possible. The goal is to make them easy to learn from.

## 1. Major Sections

Use clear numbered headings for major topics when appropriate.

Example:

## 1. Main Topic

## 2. Next Topic

## 3. Next Topic

Do not create excessive headings for tiny details.

## 2. Short Explanations

Prefer short explanations over long paragraphs.

Most concepts should be explained in approximately one to three sentences unless more detail is needed.

Avoid large walls of text.

## 3. Visual Hierarchy

Make the most important information easy to see.

Use:

- clear headings
- bold key terms
- short paragraphs
- bullets
- numbered steps
- tables
- arrows
- key insight sections
- simple text diagrams
- white space

## 4. Definitions

Format important terms clearly.

Example:

**Lead Time:** The amount of time between placing an order and receiving it.

Keep the technical term when it matters, but explain it simply.

## 5. Bullets

Use bullets when information naturally forms a group.

Example:

### Main Causes

- Supplier delays
- Transportation issues
- Forecasting errors
- Inventory shortages

Do not turn simple lists into long paragraphs.

## 6. Tables

Use a table when it makes a comparison easier to understand.

Example:

| Level | Purpose | Time Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | Long-term direction | Years |
| Tactical | Mid-term execution | Months |
| Operational | Daily execution | Days or weeks |

Do not force everything into tables.

## 7. Relationships

Show important relationships directly.

Examples:

**Higher demand → More inventory pressure**

**More competition → Lower pricing power**

Use arrows when they make cause-and-effect or sequence easier to understand.

## 8. Processes and Hierarchies

When a concept has stages, levels, or a flow, show it visually when useful.

Example:

Forecast
↓
Order
↓
Receive
↓
Store
↓
Sell

## 9. Key Insights

When a topic has one especially important takeaway, separate it.

### Key Insight

The main idea should appear here in one or two clear sentences.

## 10. Examples

Preserve useful examples from the professor or source.

Format them separately when helpful.

**Example**

A company that relies on one supplier may face major delays if that supplier shuts down.

## 11. Important Statements

A short sentence may stand alone when it captures an important idea.

*The goal is to reduce uncertainty, not eliminate it completely.*

## 12. White Space

Do not overcrowd the page.

Separate major ideas so the student can quickly scan the notes.

## 13. Language

Use simple college-level language.

Avoid:

- unnecessary jargon
- overly academic wording
- long sentences
- repeated explanations
- filler
- excessive casual language

## 14. Detail Level

Preserve:

- definitions
- formulas
- dates
- important numbers
- frameworks
- examples
- processes
- comparisons
- exceptions
- cause-and-effect relationships
- professor emphasis

Remove:

- repeated wording
- filler
- unnecessary slide text
- decorative information
- sentences that add no useful meaning

## 15. Visual Material

If a diagram, chart, image, or model helps explain the concept, include a concise explanation or text representation.

Do not describe irrelevant visual details such as colors or decoration unless they carry meaning.

## 16. Style Reference Rule

Reference screenshots and example notes are used **only to learn the preferred style**.

Never reuse facts, terminology, examples, or subject matter from a style reference unless the same information appears in the student's actual course material.
